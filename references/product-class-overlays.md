# Scope by product type — where the core applies at full strength, and where it is remapped

The ladder, gulfs, channels-as-meaning, ledger, motion grammar, and review protocol are universal. Two things vary by product class — the channel *map* (what position, heat, glow encode here) and the definition of *cost*. Read this when the product is not a task/decision tool, before applying Step 4.5 or 4.6.

- **Task / decision products** (ops, fintech, admin SaaS, productivity, e-commerce checkout): full strength, as written. Interaction is cost; the concrete channel assignments in Step 4.5 and all of Step 4.6 apply.
- **Consumption / social products** (feeds, media, community): the meta-rules hold; the concrete assignments don't. There is no commit, heat has no SLA, one-glow-per-screen is meaningless on a feed. Re-derive the channel map from the product's actual meanings before applying 4.5, and skip 4.6. Attention is the revenue here, so the ethics of "reduce interaction cost" changes sign — reducing friction toward compulsive consumption is not a win; the ledger should count engagement mechanics the way it counts elements.
- **Learning, games, fitness** (anywhere effort is the product): split the surface. Chrome around the core loop (navigation, enrollment, resume, finding the next thing) gets full strength — most of these products fail exactly there. Inside the core loop the central bet inverts: effortful interaction IS the product (desirable difficulties — retrieval beats re-reading because it is harder), so never infer away or smooth the struggle. Friction there is deliberate, budgeted like an element, and measured in retention or mastery, not clicks.
- **Persuasion artifacts** (landing/marketing pages): mostly out of scope for the interaction rules; Hick's law on calls-to-action is about all that transfers. This is the same boundary SKILL.md's Precedence section draws for conversion surfaces — the style layer may optimize first impressions there, and only there.

Extend by overlay, not by generalizing the core: when a new product class enters real design rounds, write a ~60-line overlay (channel remap + cost redefinition + class-specific regression items), hardened against that product's reviews. An overlay fighting the core is a separate skill.

## Domain constraints that move the ladder

Product class sets the channel map and the cost model. A few *domains* additionally carry constraints that override the skill's defaults — not the palette, the ladder itself. Check this table whenever the product touches money, health, safety, the state, children, or private matters. Each row names the default it overrides and what replaces it.

| Domain | Default it overrides | What applies instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Money movement** (payments, transfers, trading, payroll) | "Undo beats confirm" | Determine when the effect becomes irrevocable and what authority, review, authentication, receipt, audit, and failure handling the product and jurisdiction require. Present the amount, destination, timing, and material consequence before the irrevocable step; do not imply that a post-action UI undo can reverse an external settlement. |
| **Health & safety** (clinical, medication, dosing, allergies) | Rungs 2–3: infer from history, act automatically | The cost of a wrong guess may be bodily. Shift toward scope-down, verification, and authoritative sources. Never carry history across a patient or context boundary without validated identity and a domain-approved rule. Administration workflows follow clinical safety requirements and usability validation rather than a generic one-tap or confirmation preference. |
| **Public sector & essential services** (government, utilities, benefits) | Craft guidance treated as optional polish | Identify the applicable accessibility and plain-language laws for the jurisdiction; confirmed violations are release blockers. Preserve multiple accessible input modalities while keeping one coherent task model. Avoid engagement mechanics that obstruct access to an essential service. |
| **Regulated disclosures & consent** (finance, insurance, legal, privacy) | "Explanatory UI is a smell"; rung 0 eliminate | Copy, review, acknowledgement, and consent required by the applicable regulation cannot be inferred away or hidden in a default. Verify the actual requirement rather than assuming every disclosure needs the same treatment. Record the governing source and preserve approved legal text. |
| **Safety-critical operations** (industrial control, aviation, energy, medical devices) | Minimize or remove modes by default | Follow the governing human-factors standard and validated operating model. Make system state unmistakable, prevent focus-blind or wrong-target commands, grade alarms by urgency, and test transitions under realistic workload. Do not assume that a quasimode is always safer than an explicit maintained mode. |
| **Children's products** | Rung 2 (learn from history) | Identify the applicable age, jurisdiction, consent, and data-minimization requirements before using history or personalization. Avoid manipulative engagement patterns; use age-appropriate language, targets, and comprehension testing rather than a universal size multiplier. |
| **Privacy-sensitive surfaces** (HR, mental health, medical records, whistleblowing, adult content) | "Periphery before center" — status glanceable | Glanceable status leaks to onlookers. The distance-and-attention question (Step 1) includes *who else can see the screen*; sensitive state is deliberately **not** peripheral or ambient, and must be summoned on request (rung 5). |
| **Shared devices & multi-tenant surfaces** (kiosks, POS, clinical workstations, family devices) | Rung 2 keyed to the device | History belongs to the person, not the device: inference needs identity, and a session boundary is an event that resets the environment. No "last value used" without knowing whose last value it was. |

These rows are deliberately few. They change the *rung* an interaction lands on; they never prescribe a style. A domain that needs a palette rule is a style-layer question and belongs downstream.
